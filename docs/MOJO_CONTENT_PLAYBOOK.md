# My role in Mojo

The aim is simple: make Mojo understandable and trustworthy to the people
who use it. I write and structure the words. I do not decide what the
product does medically, legally or technically.

# Project 3 · Voice and tone guide

*Reviewer: project lead.*

### Four principles

-   **Calm.** Short sentences. No exclamation marks, no capital-letter
    alarms, never "Don't panic".

-   **Clear.** One idea and one action per message. Always say what
    happens next.

-   **Respectful.** Adults managing their own health. No blame: "Dose
    not confirmed", not "You forgot".

-   **Honest.** Never promise an outcome. Say what the strap can and
    cannot do.

### Plain-language rules

-   Sentences of 15 words or fewer. Active voice. Verb first on buttons
    ("Confirm", "Try again").

-   Digits for numbers and times: "30 seconds", "8:00 am". Dates as "23
    Oct".

-   Write translation-ready: no idioms, no slang, no jokes. Flag
    anything that may not translate.

-   **Notification privacy rule:** lock-screen text never names a
    condition or medicine. Use "Time for your 8:00 dose", and show
    details only after unlock. This matters most for conditions people
    keep private.

# Quality checklist for every piece of copy

-   **Plain language:** 15 words or fewer per sentence, one idea, one
    action, a button that starts with a verb.

-   **Claims:** every statement matches the Claims list for its release.
    Nothing implies diagnosis or detection.

-   **Consent:** anything touching location, health details or contacts
    has its own screen and a way to withdraw.

-   **Length:** fits the strap budget or the notification limit.

-   **Accessibility:** works without colour, readable aloud by a screen
    reader, large text friendly.

-   **Translation-ready:** no idioms or slang.

-   **Tested:** read aloud to at least five people from the target
    audience before launch, including one older adult for Concept A
    copy.

-   **Logged:** reviewer names and dates are in the approval log.
